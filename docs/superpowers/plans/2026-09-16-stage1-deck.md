## Ground rules (inline; you have no other context)

- **Every number on a slide has a row in `deck/claims.md`.** Add a row if you introduce a number. 🔴 rows
  get `<span class="est">team estimate</span>` next to the number on the slide, every time it appears.
  🟢/🟡 numbers get a `<sup class="ref">R#</sup>` pointing at the References slide.
- Do not invent facts, vendors, papers, or numbers. If a slide needs a fact that is not in `claims.md` or
  `hackathon-topics.md`, leave a precise placeholder in `claims.md` marked `verify` and phrase the slide

  amber / red always appear with a word (Low / Medium / High), never colour alone.

- Body ≤ 20 slides; appendix slides carry `class="slide appendix"`.
- Commit as you go on `main` (`git add <paths>`, never `-A` with stray files), push after each green

## Storyline and per-slide content

**1 Title** (`class="slide title"`). "LineSafe" · sub: "Continuous REBA/RULA ergonomic risk scoring at
the workstation — offline, skeleton-only, on an ASUS UGen300." Meta: Battlefield Lightning · Workplace
AI · Onsite track · team name placeholder `TEAM_NAME` (not a TODO; Wayne fills it) · date Oct 2026.
Full-width `.ruler` 1–15 with labels under it (Negligible 1 · Low 2–3 · Medium 4–7 · High 8–10 · Very high 11–15).

**2 Problem** — title: "Ergonomic risk is assessed with a clipboard, once, by one person." Left: how a
REBA/RULA assessment happens today (an OSH manager watches a station for ~1 h, fills a paper checklist,
files it monthly — claims 12 🔴). Right: three consequences as short statements with a one-line
explanation each — *Subjective* (two assessors, two scores), *Sampled* (the risky posture happens in the
95 % of the shift nobody watches), *No history* (nothing to show the insurer, the auditor, or the
worker). Close with one sentence: WMSDs are the largest category of occupational disease claims (no
number — we have no verified figure; do not add one).

**3 Why now** — title: "Law, insurance and the labour shortage all push the same way." Three columns:
OSH Act duty to identify and control ergonomic hazards (R7); occupational-accident insurance experience
rating rewards lower accident rates (R8); Taiwan 2025: 251 major-accident deaths, down from 287 (R6) —
plants that cannot hire more OSH staff need coverage per head. Keep it factual; no exclamation.

**4 Who pays** — title: "The buyer is the EHS department, and it already has the budget line." Table:
Customer (manufacturers ≥ 200 staff; EHS consultants as a tool; insurers as underwriting evidence) ·
Decision-maker (plant manager / EHS lead / certified OSH manager) · Budget line (safety equipment &
monitoring; training) · Reason to pay (accident cost & downtime, insurance rate, compliance). Bottom
panel: pricing shape — hardware + one-time deployment service per station, optional support.

**5 Solution in one picture** — title: "A USB stick turns any station PC into an ergonomics assessor."
One large SVG: camera → host PC + UGen300 → pose skeleton → joint angles → REBA/RULA rule engine →
score timeline with green/amber/red bands → two outputs: live alert on the station screen, and
weekly/monthly report on the manager's phone over LAN. Annotate "no raw video stored", "no cloud".

**6 Methodology** — title: "Pose to score in four deterministic steps." Steps (this IS a sequence, so
`.steps` is allowed, 4 columns): (1) `yolov8m_pose` COCO-17 keypoints at 30 FPS per person (R3) ·
(2) joint angles by geometry: trunk flexion, neck flexion, upper-arm elevation, lower-arm angle, knee
flexion, trunk twist via shoulder/hip width ratio · (3) REBA table lookup (Hignett & McAtamney 2000,
R10) and RULA (R11), with per-station load/coupling set at calibration · (4) activity score over time:
static > 1 min, repetition > 4/min, rapid change → events with timestamps. Under the steps, the
`.ruler` and one honest line: "2D pose cannot measure wrist flexion, load or grip — see slide 12."

**7 Rules, not an LLM** — title: "The live path is a rule engine; the LLM only writes the report."
Two columns. Left "Live path (every frame)": pose → angles → tables → alert; deterministic, auditable,
explains every score by the angle that caused it, runs at camera rate. Right "Report path (weekly)":
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct on the same UGen300 (R4, 7.35 tok/s, 2048-token context) turns the event
table into a plain-language summary in the plant's language; 30–60 s per report, off the live path.
Bottom line: "REBA is a lookup table. An LLM would be slower and could be wrong; a table cannot."

**8 Deployment** — title: "It hangs on the line like a sensor, and the video never leaves the room."
SVG deployment picture: station camera (USB webcam or IP cam) → existing industrial PC or mini-PC with
UGen300 on USB-C 10 Gbps → SQLite on the host → LAN → phone/tablet dashboard; a crossed-out cloud.
Right side small table: what the customer provides (a PC with a USB-C 10 Gbps port, power, one
network drop, an EHS contact) and the deployment steps with durations from `hackathon-topics.md` §1.5
(site survey 1 day; install 1–2 days; calibration 3–5 days; parallel run with manual scoring 2 weeks;
training 1 day) — those durations are 🔴, mark once with `.est` on the table header.

**9 Software architecture** — title: "Five small modules, one interface to the accelerator." SVG or
column layout: `capture` (OpenCV) → `pose` (`PoseBackend` interface: Hailo `.hef` in production,
Ultralytics on the dev laptop, replay from recorded keypoints for tests) → `angles` (pure geometry,
unit-tested against hand-built poses) → `reba` / `rula` (pure tables, unit-tested against published
worked examples) → `events` (time windows, SQLite) → `ui` (local web page: live overlay + dashboard) and
`report` (LLM on device). Include a 10-line JSON event example in `<pre>`:
`{"station":"S3","t":"2026-10-02T14:32:07","reba":9,"band":"high","drivers":["trunk_flexion 64°","upper_arm 95°"],"duration_s":38}`.
Note: no face recognition, no person ID, events are per station position.

**10 Why UGen300** — title: "2.5 W, always on, and the models are already compiled." Three facts with
big figures (`.big`): 2.5 W typical (R1); 40 TOPS INT4 / 20 TOPS INT8 with 8 GB (R1); ready `.hef` for
`yolov8m_pose`, `hand_landmark_lite`, Qwen2.5-1.5B (R3, R4). Right panel: the honest framing from the
research — the value is low-power continuous multi-stream inference, not peak LLM speed (cite the
independent 1.87 W measurement, R5 🟡). One line on why not cloud: video export, bandwidth, and
consent.

**11 Privacy by design** — title: "We keep the skeleton and throw the pixels away." Left: what is stored
(17 keypoints per frame for the last N seconds in RAM; events with station, time, score, drivers in
SQLite) and what is never stored (raw frames, faces, identities). Right: what a manager sees — "Station
3 · 14:32 · REBA 9 High · trunk flexion 64° for 38 s" — and what they cannot see (who). Add the
`.ruler`. One line on worker acceptance: the system rates the task design, not the person.

**12 Feasibility evidence** — title: "The pose front half already runs; the scoring tables are next."
Left: what exists today — a working pose pipeline from our previous project on the dev laptop:
`yolov8m-pose` ≈ 48 FPS at 1080p on Apple M4 Pro (claim 23 🟢 own measurement), One-Euro smoothing,
gap filling, a Hailo `.hef` adapter written against the pyHailoRT 5.3 API (not yet run on hardware —
say so). Right: what the Stage I video will show (laptop demo: volunteer lifts a box; score goes

**13 Validation plan** — title: "We will publish agreement with human assessors, not just FPS." Table

scripted postures. Bottom: how the parallel-run in deployment (slide 8) reuses the same protocol.
